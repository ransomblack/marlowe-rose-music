---
title: All Talk, No Action
date: 2026-10-22T00:30:00-04:00
---
I spent a lot of years talking about the things I wanted to change in my life.

I had plans. Ideas. Things I wanted to try. Things I wanted to do differently. And I could talk about them all day long.

But actually doing something about them? That was another story.

The uncomfortable truth is that I think part of me already knew I wasn't going to follow through.

Not because I didn't want those things badly enough. I did. But wanting something and believing you're capable of making it happen are two very different things.

And I didn't have much faith in myself.

I was afraid of starting something and giving up. Afraid of putting in the effort and not seeing results. Afraid of getting my hopes up only to find myself right back where I'd started.

But more than anything, I think I was afraid of letting myself down.

Again.

Because when you've spent enough time making promises to yourself that you don't keep, eventually you start questioning whether you can trust yourself at all.

So I stayed in the planning stage.

I'd think about what I wanted. Imagine what life might look like if I actually did it. Sometimes I'd even get excited enough to convince myself that *this time* would be different.

But there was always a reason to wait.

A better time. A better plan. A little more confidence.

And the longer I waited, the easier it became to convince myself that maybe those things just weren't meant for me.

Looking back now, I realize that talking about changing my life gave me just enough hope to feel like I was doing something about it.

But I wasn't.

As long as I was only talking about it, I couldn't fail at it.

But I couldn't change anything, either.

And that's a hard thing to admit.

I wish I could tell you there was one big moment when everything suddenly clicked. When I woke up full of confidence and decided I was finally going to become a different person.

But that's not really how it happened.

I didn't suddenly stop being afraid. I didn't magically become disciplined or confident. And I certainly didn't have everything figured out.

I just started making some different choices.

And somewhere along the way, those choices began adding up.

When I look at how far I've come over the last few years, I sometimes wonder what my life would look like if I'd kept putting everything off.

There are things I've done that the old me wouldn't have believed I was capable of. Things I've tried that I probably would have talked myself out of before.

And I'm proud of that.

Not because I've become someone who never struggles or doubts herself.

Believe me, I still do.

But I'm learning that I don't have to be completely sure of myself to keep going.

I'm still figuring things out. Still making plans I don't always follow perfectly. Still occasionally getting in my own way.

The difference is that I'm not content to spend the rest of my life just talking about the things I want to do.

I want to actually live them.

And at 42, I feel like I'm finally starting to do that.

**I'm still showing up.**

And I'm still discovering what I'm capable of.

— Marlowe Rose 🌹
