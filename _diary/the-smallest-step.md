It's funny how something that seems like such a small decision at the time can end up changing so much.

You don't always recognize those moments when they're happening.

Sometimes they're not even particularly exciting. There's no big announcement, no sudden burst of confidence, no feeling that your whole life is about to change.

Sometimes you just decide to try something.

And that's it.

I think for a long time I believed that if I was ever going to make real changes in my life, I'd have to become a different kind of person first.

Someone more confident. More disciplined. Someone who wasn't so afraid of doing things wrong or looking foolish.

I kept waiting to feel ready.

And if you've read my last diary entry, you already know how well that worked out for me.

But eventually, I made a decision to give myself a chance.

I didn't know whether I'd stick with it. I didn't know how far I'd get. And I certainly didn't imagine all the things that would come from it.

I was still the same person who had spent years talking herself out of things.

The only difference was that this time, I actually tried.

And then I tried again.

The funny thing is, I wasn't really thinking about all the ways my life might change. I was focused on the one thing right in front of me.

I couldn't see how that decision might affect the way I felt about myself, or how it might give me the courage to try something completely unrelated.

I definitely couldn't have imagined some of the things I'd eventually find myself doing.

But that's what happened.

Somewhere along the way, those little decisions started adding up.

Not just in the ways I could see, but in the way I started thinking about myself.

Every time I did something I hadn't believed I could do, it became a little harder to keep telling myself I wasn't capable.

And that changed more than I expected.

I started saying yes to things I might have avoided before. Trying things without needing to know whether I'd be good at them. Letting myself want things without immediately coming up with a dozen reasons they probably wouldn't work out.

And maybe the biggest surprise was realizing that I actually liked the person I was becoming.

Not because I was suddenly more accomplished or had everything figured out. But because I was finally giving myself opportunities to find out what I could do.

I still get nervous. I still overthink things. And there are plenty of days when I have to convince myself to get out of my own way.

But when I look back now, I realize how many things in my life can be traced to that one decision to try.

At the time, it didn't seem like much.

I had no idea where it would lead.

Maybe that's what makes it so special to me now.

I didn't need to know how everything was going to turn out.

I just needed to give myself a chance.

And I'm so glad I did.
